# PRD Quality Review

**Verdict: Not decision-ready.** The product direction is coherent, but delivery cannot safely begin until the unresolved access model, order/publication binding, operational calendar, and GDPR/email rules are converted into explicit requirements and acceptance criteria.

## Findings

| Location | Finding | Required decision or acceptance rule | Consequence if unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5, 6.2, 9 | The customer-facing offer is ambiguous: publications are immutable snapshots, while customers are said to consult "disponibilites"; no rule binds an order to a publication, current availability, or a dated offer. | Define the public URL/data source, whether an order records a publication snapshot/version, and the behavior when the current offer changes after the customer opens it. Test opening an old link, publishing a new offer, and submitting from each surface. | Customers can order items or prices that the farmer did not intend to offer. |
| 6.3-6.4 | A classic order requires an occurrence "lorsque pertinent", but fixed-location retrieval has no occurrence model; recurring markets and tours also lack creation/generation, timezone, cutoff, capacity, and cancellation rules. | Define one dated fulfillment instance for every selectable retrieval, its lifecycle, how instances are created, and which instances may accept/retain orders. Test past, cancelled, concurrent, and cutoff-boundary selections. | Orders cannot be reliably assigned to the preparation and distribution day. |
| 6.3, 8 | FR-026 says that final quantity and final amount are frozen at order creation, but FR-022 and FR-027 require them to be set during preparation; client edits and administrator edits have no recalculation/audit rule. | Specify immutable fields at creation versus at preparation, calculation/rounding/unit rules, manual-override provenance, and the client-visible amount at each status. Test quantity changes, zero quantity, an override, and later product-price edits. | Totals and historical records can conflict, making distribution disputes unresolvable. |
| 6.6 | AMAP generation is described only as "progressive" before an unspecified configurable horizon. The PRD does not say which occurrence is selected, when composition and replacement eligibility freeze, or what happens after suspension, cession, withdrawal change, subscription change, or failed regeneration. | Define deterministic generation triggers/idempotency, occurrence selection, freeze points, and reconciliation rules for every member action. Test repeated jobs and each action before and after generation. | Duplicate, missing, or stale AMAP baskets can be prepared or consumed. |
| 6.6 | AMAP consumption is defined for `Livree`, but reversal/correction is not. FR-072 does not define permitted transitions, an authorization rule, or whether a cancelled/reported basket restores an entitlement. | Define a state-transition matrix for classic and AMAP orders, including cancellation/report after preparation or delivery, consumption reversal, and immutable audit events. Test every allowed and rejected transition and the resulting balance. | Basket balances can be silently lost or counted twice. |
| 6.7, NFR-005 | "Persons authorized" is not an access-control design. Administrator authentication, role granularity, account recovery, session expiry, staff offboarding, and AMAP-member authorization are unspecified. | Define personas, authentication method, authorization matrix, session/security lifecycle, and audit actor identity. Test that an anonymous user, another member, and removed staff cannot access personal data or mutate records. | Personal data and order links may be accessible beyond the intended recipient. |
| 6.2, 9 | The consent scope has no subscription journey: FR-013 targets "utilisateurs inscrits", but classic checkout is accountless and no requirement lets a person subscribe, identify themselves, or receive confirmation. Audience segmentation is explicitly open. | Define subscriber identity/deduplication, collection UI and wording, opt-in confirmation policy, recipient selection, resubscription, and unsubscribe behavior. Test all consent states before every campaign send. | The team cannot determine a lawful, complete recipient list. |
| 6.2, NFR-003-004, 9 | Consent evidence is underspecified. There is no required evidence schema (text/version, timestamp, source, identity, policy version), no suppression rule for duplicate addresses, and no handling of email delivery failures or unsubscription links. | Require immutable consent events with the displayed notice/version and source; define unsubscribe/suppression and campaign-send audit data. Test withdrawal immediately before dispatch, duplicate contact data, and re-consent. | Consent cannot be demonstrated and withdrawn users can be emailed. |
| 9, NFR-003 | Core GDPR obligations are left open: retention, deletion/anonymisation, access/rectification/erasure handling, controller/processor roles, and privacy notice content. Historical immutability is not reconciled with erasure. | Decide retention schedules and lawful archival/anonymisation rules per data class, DSAR workflow/SLA, and the privacy-information surface. Define how immutable operational history is pseudonymised or retained under an applicable exception. Test export and erasure/anonymisation without corrupting aggregates. | Release may process personal data without an operable compliance process. |
| 9 | Transactional email is deferred to a future policy, although the PRD requires a secure order link to be delivered. Sender identity, trigger events, legal basis, content, retry/failure handling, and whether AMAP emails share the same rules are absent. | Enumerate every email type, trigger, recipient, required content, sender/reply handling, delivery-failure treatment, and consent/legal basis. Test that transactional mail is sent only for its event and marketing consent never gates required order access. | Essential order access can fail, or nonessential mail can be misclassified as transactional. |
| 6.1, 6.3, 6.5 | The deliberate no-reservation/no-deduction model permits demand to exceed an estimate, but there is no required customer/admin disclosure, validation behavior, or preparation exception workflow. | Define copy and behavior for over-demand, unavailable requested quantities, partial acceptance, and customer notification/approval after an adjustment. Test two simultaneous requests exceeding a visible estimate. | Customers may interpret availability as a commitment and receive unexpected substitutions or cancellations. |
| 6.5, NFR-001-002 | "Today", "tomorrow", "imminent", "progression", "anciennete inhabituelle", and "minimum d'etapes" have no definitions or measurable thresholds. | Define timezone/calendar source, each dashboard formula and threshold, required empty/error states, and task-specific maximum steps or completion-time target. Test boundary times, no-work days, empty queues, and each responsive breakpoint. | The main operational screen cannot be consistently implemented or accepted. |
| 6.7, NFR-006 | Auditability is asserted but not defined: significant actions, captured before/after values, actor identity, timestamp/timezone, reason requirements, immutability, and audit access are unspecified. | Define an audit-event schema and enumerate auditable operations, including manual amount/status/balance corrections and consent changes. Test that a correction produces a non-editable, attributable event. | The history will not explain operational or privacy-critical changes. |

## Decision Gate

Before implementation, resolve the access-control model; public offer and fulfillment-instance model; classic/AMAP state and accounting transitions; the complete email/consent inventory; and the GDPR retention/rights model. Turn the remaining qualitative requirements into measurable acceptance criteria before estimating the operational dashboard.
